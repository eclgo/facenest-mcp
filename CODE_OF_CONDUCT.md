# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our standard

We work with people who are building software around their own photos, on their own machine.
Contributions to this repository arrive as issues, pull requests, documentation fixes and
questions. In all of them:

- Be direct and specific. A report that says which tool, which arguments and what came back is
  worth more than a opinion about the project.
- Assume the reader has not seen your machine, your library, or your client. Give versions and
  steps, in the form the issue template asks for.
- Do not paste other people's paths, photo names, the endpoint file, or its token into a public
  space. Describe them.
- Criticise the code, not the person who wrote it, and not the person reporting a bug.
- Do not use this project's channels to advertise a product, a service, or a list of links.

We do not police tone beyond that. We do police the line between a report and a promotion, and
between a bug report and a leak of somebody's private data.

## Moderation and enforcement

Reports about conduct in this repository go to **support@facenest.net**, subject starting with
`conduct:`. Reports stay private; the reporter's name is not published.

What we can do, depending on what happened:

1. A private note pointing at the part of a message that crossed the line.
2. Editing, moving or deleting a public issue, comment, or pull request comment.
3. A temporary pause on opening issues or pull requests.
4. A permanent ban from the repository.

Repeated behaviour, public attacks on a named person, and publishing someone's private data are
treated as serious from the first report.

## Scope

This applies in this repository's issues, pull requests, and in any channel used to talk about
this adapter. It does not apply to how you use your own photo library, and it does not give
anyone a claim on the FaceNest app's own support channels.

## Attribution

This code of conduct is based on the Contributor Covenant, version 2.1, and is deliberately
shorter than it: the rules here are the ones that matter for a small local-first tool with a
public repository.
